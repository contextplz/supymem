# Your first venture

A venture keeps your idea, conversation, sources, documents, and progress together. Start with a task you can inspect.

## Open your app

Run `./start`, `.\start.ps1` on Windows, or open your installed app. Choose sample mode for fictional examples, or save your model on **Settings > Keys and connections > Your AI provider** and choose **Test your connection**. Keys never go in chat. Use [troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md) if it fails.

## Describe your idea

Choose **New venture**. Tell Charlie what you want to make, who would use it, the problem, and your constraints. Ask for a summary, inspect it, and request corrections. You do not need a company name first.

## Research one question

Ask a narrow audience or problem question. Built-in search and reading need no key. Keep **Search service** on **Built-in** under **Your search and page reader** unless you choose an optional provider. Check sources and dates. For business products, separate users from buyers; for consumer products, look for relevant people and communities rather than assuming business buyers.

## Plan an experiment

Ask for a small experiment with a measurable result. Your request authorizes that work; saving credentials or enabling a switch authorizes no unrelated work. **Settings > Capabilities** opens **Your capabilities** and names missing fields to complete on Keys and connections.

## Known issues

You can ask Charlie to find customers or run a scan. Partial leads, failed page builds, and proposed scans are not completed work. See [what to check before retrying](../../README.md#known-issues).

## Build when it is your next step

Charlie decides when a build is the right next step and offers it. In **Settings > Keys and connections > Coding tools**, follow the [coding-tool setup and supported builds](../../README.md#build-with-your-own-coding-tool) using your own login.

When you ask, Charlie writes the brief, starts your tool, watches it, checks the result and shows it. Your tool builds your landing pages, product, fixes and small tools on your laptop using your own subscription. Supymem does not write your code. Start with a static page or small local web app; ask Charlie to split a larger product into smaller steps.

Keep your app open. Watch or stop the build in **Build activity** and choose **Open result folder** in your desktop app. Without a connected tool, Charlie offers [the setup step](keys.md#coding-tools) instead. Nothing is published unless you ask.

## Use only the accounts you need

For requested outreach to eligible contacts, save SMTP, mailbox credentials, sender name, and postal address under **Your mail account**. Test outgoing mail and enable **Send email**. Add IMAP and **Test incoming mail** for replies. Enter an existing HTTPS booking link for invitations. Every outreach message retains disclosure, opt-out, postal address, suppression, permission checks, and caps; guessed emails stay off. A mail connection does not promise a booked call.

For an existing validated page, save and test your publishing account, enable **Publish pages**, and request deployment. Test its public URL and signup destination before sharing. Email-based signup opens a visitor's mail app, not an automatic signup record.

For another app, save your Composio project and authorization IDs, enable **Connected apps**, and authorize the account on **Connections** before requesting an action. Record results, choose your next question, set an optional monthly spend cap and provider budget, and [protect a backup](backups-security.md).
